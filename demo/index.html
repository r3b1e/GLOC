The glacier snout, as the AI sees it

Cyan line: the AI boundary. Red dot: the terminus the pipeline finds automatically along the glacier's flow line (dotted). The dot moves up-valley as the tongue shortens.

Distances are measured along the flow line from the 2017 position. 2017 and 2019 have only two clear images each, so those two positions are the least certain; the 2021→2025 retreat is the robust result. Even years (grey label) were M2 training years and are shown for continuity only.

Pipeline · runs end to end without manual steps

How a boundary is made

Find clear imagesAll Sentinel-2 passes Aug–Sep, clouds detected pixel by pixel over the glacier.
Line them upImages co-registered on stable rock to within 1.6 m.
Add terrainCopernicus 30 m elevation and slope as extra input channels.
Run GlaViTUTransformer + U-Net gives a glacier probability for every 10 m pixel.
Combine the yearMedian over 2–6 images removes snow and cloud effects.
Clean & measureBoundary polygon, area, terminus position, year of loss.
What the data can and cannot show yet

The terminus retreat for 2021–2025 is detected by every model. Total glacier area changes by about ±1 km² from year to year because fresh snow and debris hide the edges, which is more than the real 2017–2025 loss, so area loss over this short period is not yet statistically significant.

Data: Copernicus Sentinel-2 L1C, Copernicus DEM GLO-30, RGI 7.0 / GLIMS. Model: GlaViTU (Maslov et al., Nature Communications 2025), fine-tuned in this project.
